Judging Criteria & Submission
Judging Criteria
Projects will be evaluated on the following four dimensions:
1. Learning & Understanding (30%)
Can you explain what you built and why? Do you understand the code you wrote? This is what sets MathHacks apart from other hackathons. We reward hackers who genuinely learned something, even if their project is simple. AI tools are allowed, but you should be able to explain everything you submit. If you can teach a judge something about your project, you're in great shape.
2. Application of Mathematics (25%)
How meaningfully does the project incorporate math concepts? Is math central to the project? Does the project demonstrate understanding of the math involved? We want to see that you are using math in a thoughtful, well-implemented way.
3. Technical Implementation (25%)
Does your project actually work? How well is the code written? Did you use the right tools for the job? Judges understand you had limited time, so we're not expecting perfection. We're looking for thoughtful technical decisions and working functionality.
4. Creativity & Originality (20%)
How unique is your idea? Did you approach the problem in a way that surprised the judges? This isn't about complexity. A simple idea executed in a clever way scores higher than a complicated project that copies an existing product.
Judging Format
Judging is in person, science-fair style, at your table. Teams are split into three groups by team number, and each group presents during a one-hour block:
Group A: 1:45-2:45 PM
Group B: 2:50-3:50 PM
Group C: 3:55-4:55 PM
Your group will be communicated during the event. During your block, two different judges will each visit your table for about 8 minutes: a 3-minute demo, questions (every member should expect one), and feedback. Your whole team should stay at your table for your entire block. Outside it, explore other teams’ projects or join mini-events.
Tips for a Great Demo
Start with the problem. What does your project solve, and who is it for?
Show, don’t tell. Demo the actual project on your laptop. Skip the slides.
Know your code. Judges may ask you to walk through any part of it.
Explain your math. What did you use, and why?
Highlight what you learned. Especially if you’re a beginner, tell judges what you knew Saturday morning vs. what you know now.
Submission Instructions
Deadline: 1:00 PM on Sunday, October 18, 2026.
Method: Submission details will be shared at the opening ceremony.
What to include:
Project name
Team number and member names
Brief description (2 to 3 sentences)
Link to source code (GitHub, Replit, etc.)
Optional: link to a deployed/live version
Your project must be built during the event. Pre-existing projects are not allowed. You can use open-source libraries, frameworks, and APIs, but the core project must be new.
Disqualification
Projects may be disqualified for:
Being started before the event
Plagiarism or copying another team's work
Violating the Code of Conduct
Not submitting by the deadline
Not being at your table during your judging block
